# Building the post from the references, element by element

The post is a set of choices. For each element, take the version that won in the research, name the post it comes
from, and give its numbers. A reader should be able to check every row against the reference itself.

## The move that makes the combination better than any one reference

No reference gets everything right. Look for the split between reach and saves:

- The post that **travelled furthest** (highest plays ÷ followers) usually has the cover and hook to copy.
- The post that was **saved most** (saves per 1,000 plays) usually has the content and number format to copy.

When they're different posts, take the cover from the first and the slides from the second. When one post leads on
both, keep what it does and fix its weak spots (a cover you shouldn't copy, numbers off the source, a missing save ask)
from the other references and the niche-wide patterns. In the Weight Class run,
@uplejaa's "Easy swaps that ACTUALLY save calories" reached 159× her followers but was saved 5 times per 1,000 plays
(calories per 100 g). @laurenjoyke11y's per-serving numbers were saved 40 times per 1,000 but reached 25×. The post
used uplejaa's cover words with laurenjoyke11y's numbers.

## The elements

Go through each one. "What won" comes from `refs.md` and `formulas.md`, so cite the post and its numbers.

| Element | What to decide | Where the evidence is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Formula | The promise and how each slide delivers it (one swap per slide, one order per slide) | `formulas.md`: median saves per 1,000, creators |
| Cover words | The exact phrase, which word to highlight | The formula's highest-ratio cover; cover templates in `refs.md` |
| Number on the cover | A count ("5"), a total ("368 fewer calories"), or none | "Covers with a number" in `formulas.md` |
| Cover photo | What's in it and how it's shot | The most-saved references' covers (look at the sheets) |
| Slide layout | What sits where on a content slide | The most-saved reference's slides |
| Numbers | Which numbers, per what (per serving, per order, per 100 g) | Compare saves of posts using each format |
| Photos | Real phone photos, cut-outs, packshots or designed slides | Which kind the most-saved references use |
| Verdict line | A one-line takeaway under each slide | References that use one |
| Page count | `n/N` on each slide | References that use one |
| Save ask | The words and where (cover, last slide, caption) | Asks in `formulas.md`; the most-saved posts' wording |
| Length | Number of slides | Median slides for the formula |
| Shape | 3:4, 4:5, 9:16 | Shape counts in `formulas.md` |
| The product | Where it appears and what it does there | References that end on an app or product (usually last slide only) |
| Sound | The exact sound, or "a quiet song" | Sounds under 2+ winners in `formulas.md` |
| Caption | One line plus 4–5 hashtags | The references' captions |
| Angle | What makes this one worth saving for this audience (e.g. protein on every swap) | Saves by theme, if the research has it |

## How the product earns its slide

Product videos on their own get watched and rarely kept (in the Weight Class run, calorie-counter-app videos were saved
11.3 times per 1,000 plays against 35.2 for high-protein posts). So the product rides inside a post people want to keep,
and appears once, at the end, as the answer to a question the post raises: "where do these numbers come from?",
"how do I log this order?". Never on the cover: none of the winning app accounts put it there.

## Writing the "why it will work" table

| Element | What we do | Taken from | The evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cover words | "5 easy swaps that actually save calories", with "actually" boxed | @uplejaa (link) | 178K plays on 1,124 followers: 159×, the furthest any swaps post reached |

- Link each reference the first time it appears.
- Evidence is numbers from the research (plays, followers, ratio, saves per 1,000, counts like "30 of 95 covers"),
  not adjectives.
- If an element is your own choice with no evidence behind it, say so in the row. Don't dress up a guess.
- Then list what could go wrong, honestly: small samples (say how many posts back each lead), every reference
  being a person's account when the post goes out from a brand account, generic numbers against a photographed brand's
  label, a sound that's a correlation, not a cause.

## Calling things by what they are

- A formula backed by one creator is that creator's template, not the niche's. Two posts make a lead. Three or four
  make a proven but thin formula, so say how many posts back it.
- Promotions (ads, campaigns posting one caption from many accounts, app accounts) prove a brand can ride the
  format. Count them, but don't let them set the medians.
- Accounts under 1,000 followers make enormous ratios from small numbers (25 followers, 107K plays = 4,290×). Judge
  them by saves.
