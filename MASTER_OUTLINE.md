# Personal Brand Launch: Master Outline

A synthesis of all 13 videos on [youtube.com/@PersonalBrandLaunch](https://www.youtube.com/@PersonalBrandLaunch)
(October 2025 to May 2026). The host introduces themself as Ava and teaches business owners how to
grow and sell on Instagram with short-form content.

The videos repeat a lot of material, so this outline is organized by **topic**, not by video. Each
concept has three parts:

- **What it is**: the idea in plain terms
- **How to apply it**: the steps as taught
- **Example**: taken from the videos unless marked *(illustrative)*, in which case it's one I wrote to show the idea

Source videos are listed under each heading as `#1` to `#13` (see the [video index](#video-index)).
A few things were shown on screen but never said out loud, so they are missing from the transcripts.
They're listed in [Appendix D](#appendix-d-shown-on-screen-not-in-the-transcripts).

---

## The whole system in one paragraph

Build a **personal brand, not just a following**. Post a mix of educational, storytelling and
authority content so people know, like and trust you. Don't guess what will work: borrow hooks,
topics and script structures from **outlier** videos that have already gone viral, then fill them
with **your own** knowledge, stories and results. Plan and produce content in **monthly batches**
using the **7 factors of a short**. Once you have your own hits, **double down** on them, following the
**70/20/10** rule. Optimize your profile so visitors become followers and leads. Use AI to speed up the
workflow but keep the ideas, stories and decisions human. Above all, **keep posting**, because the only
way to fail is to stop.

## Part 1: Mindset and principles

### 1.1 Build a personal brand, not a following
*Sources: #2, #5, #7, #11*

- **What it is:** There are two kinds of accounts. A *following* comes from one-off videos such as trends,
  aesthetic clips, "audio + B-roll + text" posts, or only ever one type of content. People came for the video,
  not for you, so views swing wildly and every post has to be a masterpiece. A *personal brand* means
  people know, like and trust **you**. Views stay consistent from post to post and followers turn into leads.
- **How to apply it:**
  1. Post all three content types (educational, storytelling, authority; see [3.1](#31-the-three-types-of-content)) every month.
  2. Skip trends, memes and "fluffy" content, even when they would get views.
  3. Check your grid: consistent views across posts mean a brand. Views that jump from 500 to 500K mean a following.
- **Example:** Shelby Stap, cited in #5 and #7, grew by more than 1M followers in a year by posting all three types.
  The views on that profile are very consistent and the view-to-follower ratio is strong, which is the sign of a tight community.

### 1.2 "Never throw spaghetti at the wall"
*Sources: #1, #2, #4, #7, #12*

- **What it is:** Don't guess what will work. Topics, hooks and script structures should come from
  videos that have **already** gone viral (outliers). If something went viral once, it has a much better
  chance of going viral again.
- **How to apply it:** Research before you ideate ([Part 4](#part-4-research-finding-proven-ideas)). Borrow the
  *packaging* (hook, topic, structure) but always write the *value* yourself.
- **Example:** A viral outlier hook, "This is not a flex, but I'm pretty [expletive] good at photography and
  videography", became Ava's "Not to flex, but I'm pretty [expletive] good at social media marketing." The
  hook was borrowed and the content after it was original (#4, #7, #12).

### 1.3 Borrow structure, never copy
*Sources: #4, #6, #11, #12*

- **What it is:** Using a proven hook or a fill-in-the-blank script structure is fine. Copying
  someone's whole video word for word is plagiarism, and it turns you into "AI slop".
- **How to apply it:**
  - Changing one or two words of a hook is usually fine, depending on the hook.
  - Build each hook stack from **different** outlier videos so you don't end up too close to any one creator.
  - Always fill the structure with your own knowledge, stories and results.
  - If someone copies you, make sure you were the first to post it.
- **Example:** In #4, the outlier hook "This is the best hairstyle for your face shape" became "This is the best
  hairstyle for different tops", with only the last words changed.

### 1.4 Integrity over virality
*Sources: #1, #2, #6*

- **What it is:** Don't post anything that feels "icky" or that you'll regret just to get views. Going viral
  on fluff brings followers who don't care about you and won't buy from you.
- **How to apply it:** Before posting, ask whether the viewer walks away with something that makes their
  life better, and whether the post is valuable, educational, vulnerable or real. If you reuse a trend's
  structure, make it educational or storytelling, never empty.
- **Example** *(illustrative)*: Instead of joining a trending dance, a bookkeeper posts "3 mistakes that
  cost me $10K in my first year of business."

### 1.5 Consistency and patience
*Sources: #1, #2, #5*

- **What it is:** Growth from 0 to 1,000 followers is the hardest, most miserable stage, and most people quit there.
  As long as you keep posting, you haven't failed.
- **How to apply it:** Commit to a posting schedule ([7.1](#71-posting-amount-and-schedule)). Expect roughly
  50 to 100 videos to reach 1K followers. Brand-new accounts grow more slowly than existing ones.
- **Example:** Ava's personal account got its first big hit after about 2 weeks. A brand-new business page
  took about 3 months. Chloe posted for more than 4 years before a series made the account take off, and Ken
  posted for 2 years (#5).

## Part 2: Foundations: brand and profile

Video #2 uses a **house analogy**: the *foundation* is your niche, the *interior* is you (your knowledge, stories and
results), and the *exterior* is your visual branding.

### 2.1 Niche and sub-niches (the foundation)
*Sources: #1, #2, #4, #12*

- **What it is:** One broad niche plus 5 to 7 sub-niches you'll post about, plus your occupation or title. This list
  also becomes your research keyword list.
- **How to apply it:**
  1. Write your broad niche (fitness, business, sales, beauty, fashion, and so on).
  2. List 5 to 7 sub-niches under it.
  3. Add your occupation or title.
  4. Each video's **topic** is one sub-niche.
- **Example:** Fitness → lifting, cardio, nutrition, protein, sleep, getting a six-pack (#1, #2).

### 2.2 Put the "personal" in personal brand (the interior)
*Source: #2*

- **What it is:** What makes the brand yours:
  - *Educational content* uses **your** knowledge and experience.
  - *Storytelling content* uses your personal and business stories (wins, losses, founder story).
  - *Authority content* shows your own transformation or the results you engineered for clients.
- **How to apply it:** Make three lists: topics you're an expert in, stories from your life and business, and
  results or testimonials you can show. Draw on them in every script.
- **Example** *(illustrative)*: For a fitness coach, the knowledge is how to set a calorie deficit. The stories
  are "the year I was 50 lbs heavier." The authority content is a client's 12-week before and after.

### 2.3 Visual brand and editing style (the exterior)
*Sources: #1, #2, #12*

- **What it is:** A consistent, recognizable look of colors, fonts, visuals and music, so people know it's your
  video before they see your face.
- **How to apply it:**
  1. **Colors:** pick 2 or 3, using a color rule or simply ones you like.
  2. **Fonts:** pick 2 or 3:
     - a *primary* font for written hooks and big bold text;
     - a *secondary* font for regular captions;
     - an optional *accent* font, used sparingly for annotations.
  3. **Visuals:** pick 2 or 3 types you'll use consistently, such as screenshots, screen recordings, B-roll, images,
     PNGs, or charts and graphs.
  4. Decide once, then edit every video the same way.
- **Example** *(illustrative)*: Navy, cream and orange; a bold sans-serif for hooks, a clean sans-serif for
  captions and a handwritten accent; screenshots plus B-roll in every video.

### 2.4 Profile optimization: the top half
*Sources: #2, #9*

- **What it is:** People look at your profile for about **5 seconds** before deciding whether to follow. Five
  elements decide it, and each has a wrong and a right version:

  | Element | Wrong | Right |
  |---|---|---|
  | Link in bio | A link page with many links. Too many choices means no choice, and Ava says you can "say goodbye to 50% of your conversions." | **One** link to a freebie or a product or service landing page |
  | Username | Random, long or confusing | Your name or your business name |
  | Tagline (name field) | Just your name, or a phrase nobody would search | Name + occupation or a keyword people would search, plus your city for in-person businesses |
  | Profile picture | Far away, busy background, several people, blurry | A clear close-up on a solid or plain background (pfpmaker.com, tinted to brand colors) |
  | Bio | Messy and unstructured | The 4-line framework below |

- **How to apply it: the 4-line bio** (#9)
  1. Line 1: what you do or sell, and who you help.
  2. Line 2: how you work with people, or how they buy from you.
  3. Line 3: credibility, such as results, awards or credentials.
  4. Line 4: a CTA, usually to a freebie or paid offer.
- **Example** *(illustrative)*:
  > Helping coaches get clients from short-form video
  > Done-for-you scripting + editing
  > 40M+ views generated for clients
  > Free 30-day content plan ↓

### 2.5 Highlights
*Source: #9*

- **What it is:** Five story highlights that answer a visitor's questions.
- **How to apply it:** On your profile, tap + New, select stories, name the highlight and add it. Create these five:

  | Highlight | What goes in it |
  |---|---|
  | Welcome / Start here | A short intro video and a "fun facts about me" slide |
  | Reviews / Results | Client or personal before and afters, testimonials, screenshots |
  | Packages / Offers | A breakdown of your main offer, product or service, or everything you offer |
  | Tips / Hacks / Ideas | One-off niche tips, systems and step-by-steps |
  | FAQ (optional) | Answers to questions you keep getting in comments |

  For covers, search "highlight covers" in Canva, edit a template to match your brand, then go to the highlight, tap
  ⋯, choose Edit highlight, then Edit cover.
- **Example** *(illustrative)*: A skincare brand's FAQ highlight answers "Is it safe for sensitive skin?" and
  "How long until I see results?"

### 2.6 Pinned posts
*Source: #9*

- **What it is:** Your three pinned posts are an underrated sales page. What to pin depends on your business type:

  | Business type | Pin 1 | Pin 2 | Pin 3 |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Service-based | My story or founder story | "How we work together" authority reel: your process, a real client case study, or your own before and after | Your best hyper-educational reel |
  | Product-based | My story or founder story | Product creation story: how and why it was made | A before and after using the product |
  | Educational creator | My story: how you got into what you teach | About / mission: what the page is for and your big goal | Your best educational reel |

- **How to apply it:** Make each one deliberately, using story script structures for the founder story, then pin them.
- **Example** *(illustrative)*: A web designer pins (1) "I quit my agency job with $2K in savings," (2) "Here's
  exactly what happens in our 4-week website build," and (3) "5 homepage mistakes killing your sales."

## Part 3: Content strategy: what to post

### 3.1 The three types of content
*Sources: #2, #5, #7*

- **What it is:**
  - **Educational**: niche lessons, tips, mistakes and tutorials. It builds authority through your knowledge.
  - **Personal / storytelling**: your background, founder story, wins, losses and experiences, plus lifestyle
    content showing your audience's dream result. It builds connection.
  - **Authority**: your own or your clients' results and testimonials. It builds trust and brings leads.
    Expect it to get the **fewest views**, which is normal.
- **How to apply it:** Post a mix of all three every month and never just one. Together they make the "trifecta"
  that builds a community rather than a following.
- **Example:** Shelby Stap's profile, as described in #5 and #7. The educational posts build authority, the personal
  posts build a close community, and the client results bring in leads.

### 3.2 The funnel: TOFU, MOFU, BOFU
*Source: #8*

- **What it is:** Each piece of content sits at a stage of the funnel, and the goal you have for a post decides which stage to use.

  | Stage | Goal | What it looks like | CTA |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | **TOFU** (top of funnel) | Views | Broad, easy-to-follow, simple script structures: rankings, tier lists, rapid-fire Q&A, "if you do X, Y happens" | Follow CTA, plus some freebie CTAs (these can bring a lot of leads) |
  | **MOFU** (middle of funnel) | Followers and connection | Founder story, product or service story, day in the life, personal or business challenges, wins, losses and lessons, series | Follow CTA |
  | **BOFU** (bottom of funnel) | Leads, trust and authority | In-depth tutorials, one-off tips, comparisons, personal and client before and afters, "fake case studies" ("what I'd do for [big brand]"), "what I'd do in [situation]" | Freebie CTA or paid-offer CTA |

  Be careful with straight testimonial or "buy now" videos, because short-form content isn't an ad. A better
  way to get leads is to post deeply educational content and end it with a lead CTA that includes social proof.

- **How to apply it: weekly posting split (7 posts a week)**

  | Followers | TOFU | MOFU | BOFU |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 0 to 1K | 3 (2 follow CTA, 1 freebie CTA) | 2 (follow CTA) | 2 (follow CTA) |
  | 1K to 10K | 2 (1 follow, 1 freebie) + 1 double down | 1 (follow) | 3 (2 follow, 1 paid offer) |
  | 100K to 1M\* | 2 (1 follow, 1 freebie) + 1 double down | 1 (follow) | 3 (1 follow, 2 paid offer) |

  \*The video skips straight from "1K to 10K" to "100K to 1M". It probably means "10K and up".

- **Example:**
  - TOFU: "If you drink apple cider vinegar with lemon, your belly feels flatter…"
  - MOFU: "I started my pajama business when I was 19 with $800."
  - BOFU: "If I had to create content for a fitness brand like Gymshark starting from scratch, here's what I'd do."

### 3.3 Building in public as a founder
*Source: #13*

- **What it is:** Founders need two kinds of content, so people **trust you like an expert** and **follow your journey**:
  - **Expert / authority**, 50 to 75% of posts: educational content and social proof that position you as the authority.
  - **Documentation / journey**, 25 to 50% of posts: the real process of building, including decisions,
    wins, failures and lessons, not just the finished product.
- **How to apply it:** Fill the split from these idea banks.
  - *Documentation ideas:*
    - series: daily, progress update, lessons, or step-by-step (see [3.4](#34-series-content));
    - number or metric updates, such as income or revenue;
    - my story: your life, how the business started, or how a product was built;
    - cost breakdowns;
    - "what I'd do differently" retrospectives.
  - *Expert ideas:*
    - step-by-step tutorials;
    - comparisons;
    - myth busts and common mistakes;
    - do vs don't;
    - tips and hacks;
    - Q&A: rankings, tier lists, rapid-fire;
    - transformations, of yourself or clients;
    - a fake case study for a celebrity or big brand;
    - a fake case study for a situation ("if I were starting from scratch…").

  Documentation content is harder to make entertaining. Follow strong founders online for inspiration.
- **Example:** Documentation hooks shown in #13 include "Today is day one of rehabbing my bowling alley",
  "This is how much I made in January as a full-time content creator", and "I paid $948,000 to build my dream home
  as an owner-builder; here's the complete cost breakdown." An expert hook: "If I were CEO of McDonald's…"

### 3.4 Series content
*Sources: #5, #7, #11, #13*

- **What it is:** A planned sequence of related posts with the same theme, format or promise, released over time.
  People **follow so they don't miss the next episode**, especially after a cliffhanger. It's the Netflix model
  applied to short-form video.
- **How to apply it:**
  1. **Pick the niche or topic** of the series.
  2. **Pick a series type:**
     - *Daily series*: the hook states the day and restates the goal ("Day 71 of…"), then gives that day's win,
       problem or progress. It ends when the goal is reached. It's hard to sustain, which is exactly why it stands out.
     - *Progress-update series*: one goal, with updates at irregular intervals that can follow the steps of a plan.
     - *Lesson series*: one lesson or educational take per episode.
     - *Step-by-step series* (#11, #13): each episode covers one step of a process.
  3. **Write a rough draft**: the number of episodes, each episode's premise, and a posting schedule.
  4. **Script, film, edit and post** using the formats that suit series: shot/angle change, visual (prop),
     voiceover, and setting change.
  5. End episodes on a cliffhanger when you can.
- **Example:**
  - Haley's progress-update series documents writing a book toward becoming a #1 New York Times bestseller.
  - Chloe's "30 lessons by 30" is a lesson series.
  - Ken's "raw faith" series shares one lesson from a faith journey per episode.

### 3.5 Carousels
*Source: #10*

- **What it is:** Multi-image posts. They're mostly shown to existing followers, but when they go viral they reach
  non-followers like Reels do. Eight types are getting reach right now:
  1. **Tutorial**: a step-by-step process, framework or acronym.
  2. **Do vs don't** (right vs wrong, old vs new).
  3. **Tips and hacks**: one-off niche tips.
  4. **Storytelling**: one line of the story plus a related photo per slide. Try stacking screenshots with a year-by-year text breakdown.
  5. **Transformation**: the before and after, then the steps that got you there.
  6. **Problem / solution**: a niche problem and its fix, repeated.
  7. **List**: one point per slide.
  8. **Types**: different types of one niche thing.
- **How to apply it:**
  - *Fastest viral carousel:* on your profile, open the Reels tab and sort by most viewed. Take your top reel,
    pick a carousel type, rebuild the reel as a carousel in Canva with the reel's verbal hook as the cover slide,
    and post it. Many of your followers probably came from that reel, so they'll want it.
  - *Audio hack (credited to Brock Johnson):* add a **trending audio** to the carousel. Without audio it can't
    appear in the Reels tab; with it, it has a chance, which can multiply views about 10x.
  - *When to use them:* new accounts should focus on Reels, which bring followers. Carousels work best once you have
    an audience to connect with, give value to and turn into leads.
- **Example:** Ava's year-by-year storytelling carousel got about 1.4M views and 6K followers, more views than the
  reel version of the same story.

## Part 4: Research: finding proven ideas

### 4.1 Outliers and the 5x rule
*Sources: #1, #2, #4, #6, #7, #12*

- **What it is:** An **outlier** is a video from a small or mid-size creator that massively outperforms that creator's average.
  - *Other creators' videos:* the **5x rule** says the video has at least 5 times as many (organic) views as the creator has followers.
  - *Your own videos:* an outlier is far above your own average, for example a 10K-view video when you usually get 1K.
- **How to apply it:**
  - Only save videos that meet the 5x rule, ideally posted within the last year (the more recent the better).
  - Do your research on **Instagram**, because it's the hardest platform to go viral on, so anything viral there is genuinely strong.
- **Example** *(illustrative)*: A creator with 20,000 followers has a reel with 140,000 views. That's 7x, so it's an outlier worth saving.

### 4.2 The research keyword list
*Sources: #4, #12*

- **What it is:** The search terms you'll research each month.
- **How to apply it:** Combine your niche, every sub-niche, and your occupation or title. Search variations of each.
- **Example** *(illustrative)*: For a styling niche: "styling", "body type outfits", "capsule wardrobe",
  "color analysis", "personal stylist".

### 4.3 The research methods
*Sources: #1, #2, #4, #7, #12*

- **What it is:** A repeatable monthly process for filling a saved folder with outlier hooks and topics.
- **How to apply it:**
  1. **Set up a dummy research account and reset its algorithm** (#4): Profile → ☰ menu → Content preferences →
     Reset suggested content, then confirm.
  2. **Keyword research:** Explore → search bar → enter a keyword → scroll the Reels results → save every 5x video
     with a good hook or topic. Repeat for every keyword on your list.
  3. **Creator / account research:** first build a list of accounts in your niche. Screenshot every account that fits
     what you or your client would post. Four ways to find them:
     - the *Accounts* tab for a keyword search;
     - open a good reel, then tap through to the creator's profile;
     - follow a top account, then browse its *Suggested accounts*;
     - open a top creator's *Following* list.

     Then sort each account's reels by views:
     - *on the phone:* Reels tab → dropdown → Most viewed;
     - *on desktop:* the **Sort Feed** Chrome extension → All items → Views → export CSV.

     Save that creator's 5x videos.
  4. **Reels recommendation tab ("intentional scrolling"):** after all that research, the algorithm on the
     dummy account starts feeding you niche videos that didn't show up in searches. Scroll and save the 5x ones.
     Some of the best outliers come from this step, so don't skip it.
- **Example:** Ava runs this process monthly for personal and client accounts, so a whole month of scripts can be
  batched from one research session (#4).

### 4.4 Cross-niche viral templates
*Sources: #4, #6, #7, #12*

- **What it is:** Some hook and script patterns go viral in **any** niche, so you can turn them into reusable templates.
- **How to apply it:** Use outliers from your own niche first. If you can't find one, or you love a hook from another niche,
  turn it into a template and adapt it.
- **Example:** These templates recur across many niches:
  - "Is it possible to [hard goal] in [short time]?"
  - "[600 days / 6 months / 6 years] ago, I…"
  - "Did you know if you [do X]…"
  - "Three levels of [thing]"
  - "Smart [person] vs dumb [person] when it comes to [topic]"
  - The good / better / best ladder: "Fried eggs are bad for weight loss, scrambled are good, boiled are excellent"
    became "Posting randomly is bad, 4 times a week is good, 7 times is excellent."
  - "This is a picture of my first date ever" was reused by someone in a different niche: "…with this guy named Ben."

## Part 5: Anatomy of a short: the 7 factors

*Sources: #1, #2, #12*. Every viral short is made of seven factors:

| # | Factor | Question it answers |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Topic | What is the video about? |
| 2 | Hooks | What do they hear, read and see in the first 3 seconds? |
| 3 | Value | What specific takeaway do they leave with? |
| 4 | Script angle / structure | Which direction does the video take, and how is the value organized? |
| 5 | CTA | What should they do at the end? |
| 6 | Format | How is it filmed to hold attention? |
| 7 | Editing style | What text, colors, visuals and music does it use? |

You can work through them in any order; Ava often picks hooks first. **Topic and hooks are arguably the two most important.**

### 5.1 Topic
*Sources: #1, #2, #12*

- **What it is:** What the video is about.
- **How to apply it:** Choose one sub-niche ([2.1](#21-niche-and-sub-niches-the-foundation)), or a topic taken from an outlier video.
- **Example:** Choosing "getting a six-pack" from a fitness sub-niche list (#1).

### 5.2 Hooks and hook stacks
*Sources: #1, #2, #4, #7, #12*

- **What it is:** The first 3 seconds decide how the whole video performs. There are three hooks:
  - **Verbal**: what you *hear*.
  - **Written**: the on-screen text you *read*.
  - **Visual**: what you *see*.

  A **hook stack** is all three for one video, each taken from an outlier. The more hooks you use, the better
  your chances.
- **How to apply it:**
  1. When batching, build a hook stack for every video **before** scripting.
  2. Pull the three hooks from different outlier videos.
  3. Frame each stack in one of the 7 angles ([5.4](#54-script-angle)).
- **Example:** Hook stacks Ava built for a styling niche (#4):
  - *Written:* "Hairstyle neckline guide." *Verbal:* "This is the best hairstyle for different tops." *Visual:* a clone shot.
  - *Written:* "How to dress for your proportions." *Verbal:* "How to dress for your body type." *Visual:* drawn,
    labelled body-type figures in one row.
  - *Written:* "Jeans for different body types." *Verbal:* "Try on jeans based on your body shape." *Visual:* three
    clones in three labelled pairs of jeans.

### 5.3 Value: measurable, not fluffy
*Sources: #1, #2, #12*

- **What it is:** The meat of the video, its educational takeaway. It comes from:
  - **your own knowledge and experience**, which is preferred because it's authentic and gives you credibility; or
  - an **outside source** (a study, article, expert or other video), which is fine if you **credit it**.

  **Measurable value** is specific, actionable and clear. **Fluffy value** is vague, and viewers don't know what to do with it.
- **How to apply it:** For each video, write down the single specific thing a viewer could act on today. Only post
  in areas where you actually have expertise.
- **Example:** In #1 and #2, Ava breaks down the value in fitness creator Jeff Nippard's videos:
  - a study comparing calf-raise variations;
  - a full day of eating for under $8;
  - 5 steps to find your max dead-hang time and what level that puts you at;
  - a tier list of training splits based on personal experience.

  *(illustrative)* Fluffy: "Eat healthier." Measurable: "Subtract 500 calories from your maintenance calories to lose
  about 1 lb a week."

### 5.4 Script angle
*Sources: #1, #2, #4, #10, #13*

- **What it is:** The direction a video takes. One idea can become **seven videos** through seven angles:

  | Angle | What it does |
  |---|---|
  | Tutorial | A step-by-step process, framework or acronym |
  | Comparison | Compares methods, actions or products for the same result |
  | Myth bust / common mistake | Debunks a common belief or mistake in your niche |
  | Do vs don't | Right vs wrong ways to get a result |
  | Tip / hack | One-off lesson or trick |
  | Transformation | A before and after, plus how to get the same result |
  | Challenge | Gamifies trying to get a result |

  Any angle can be told **educationally** or as a **story**.
- **How to apply it:** Pick the angle, decide whether it's educational or storytelling, and write the script, using a
  template if it helps.
- **Example** *(illustrative; the video's own list for "abs" was on screen only)*: For the topic "abs":
  - *tutorial:* "My 3-move ab routine";
  - *comparison:* "Crunches vs planks";
  - *myth bust:* "You can't spot-reduce belly fat";
  - *do vs don't:* "Stop doing sit-ups like this";
  - *tip:* "Exhale fully at the top of every rep";
  - *transformation:* "My 12-week core before and after";
  - *challenge:* "100 hollow holds a day for 30 days."

### 5.5 Script structures
*Sources: #3, #6, #7, #12*

- **What it is:** A **fill-in-the-blank template** for the body of a script. Every video has one, and "all trends
  are just repeatable script structures." There are two axes:
  - **Easy vs complex.** *Easy* structures are obvious, repetitive patterns such as rating lists or "if you do X, Y happens."
    They're great for views and reach but bring **few followers**. *Complex* structures don't look templated and tend to be
    hyper-educational or raw storytelling. They sometimes get fewer views but have a **much better view-to-follower ratio**.
    Lean toward complex structures right now.
  - **Educational vs storytelling.**
- **How to apply it:** Build a bank of structures:
  1. **Find** an outlier video (5x rule) with a structure you like.
  2. **Transcribe** it: on the reel, tap ⋯ → Copy link → paste into gettranscribe.ai → Get transcript.
  3. **Templatize** it in ChatGPT with this prompt:
     > This is a transcript from a viral video. Please make it into a script template that could be used for any
     > niche. Keep the overall format/structure of the video and just make it into a fill-in-the-blank version.
  4. **Finalize** it: paste the template into a Google Doc, adjust it, and keep it in your bank.

  When writing, combine a hook stack with a structure from the bank, then fill it with your own value.
- **Example:**
  - Ava's easy "rating" reel got more than 1M views but only about 200 followers. Other videos with 1M views brought in
    30K+ followers.
  - A complex structure that worked for Ava and clients: "Two [people] are trying to [goal]…", such as two couples
    trying to invest, or two business owners starting to post content.

### 5.6 CTA (call to action)
*Sources: #1, #2, #8, #12*

- **What it is:** What you ask viewers to do at the end. The two main goals are **followers** and **leads** (to a freebie,
  product or service). The options are a follow CTA, a ManyChat comment-keyword CTA, an engagement CTA, or no CTA.
- **How to apply it:**
  - Match the CTA to the post's funnel stage ([3.2](#32-the-funnel-tofu-mofu-bofu)).
  - For links, use **ManyChat**, not "link in bio". The viewer comments a keyword and gets the link by DM automatically.
  - *ManyChat setup:*
    1. Create a new automation.
    2. Add a new trigger: "User comments on your post or reel" (all posts, or one).
    3. Set a trigger word of about 5 to 7 letters or fewer.
    4. Write the DM, add a button with your link, and set it live.
  - Set up one automation in advance to keep it simple.
- **Example** *(illustrative)*: "Comment PLAN and I'll DM you my free 30-day content calendar."

### 5.7 Format
*Sources: #1, #2, #3, #5, #7, #12*

- **What it is:** The engaging way a video is filmed. Sitting down and talking to camera is usually boring (#12 admits
  sit-down styles are having a moment), and better formats raise watch time. The common formats:
  1. **Talking back and forth**: you play a "smart" and a "dumb" character, and the smart one teaches.
  2. **Visual**: props or visuals demonstrate the concept.
  3. **Voiceover**: footage or images with your voice over them.
  4. **Multitasking**: teaching while doing something unrelated, such as cooking, cleaning or makeup.
  5. **Setting change**: a new location every line or two.
  6. **Shot / angle change**: a new shot every ~2 seconds.
  7. **Clone**: two of you on screen comparing two things (Ava's favorite).
  8. **Whiteboard**: explaining on a whiteboard.
  9. **Q&A**: someone off camera asks one or more people a question.
  10. **Green screen**: relevant images or video behind you.
  11. **Reaction**: react to a mega-viral niche video, then give your expert take.
- **How to apply it:** Choose a format per video, sometimes before scripting, and adjust the script to fit it.
  - *Series* work best as shot/angle change, visual, voiceover or setting change.
  - *Stories* work best as voiceover, shot/angle change, or audio B-roll + text ([6.4](#64-best-formats-for-stories)).
- **Example:** In #7, Ava reuses the same hook with a different visual approach in each hook-stack example, and calls the
  clone format a personal favorite for comparisons.

### 5.8 Editing style
*Sources: #1, #2, #3*

- **What it is:** Factor 7 is the consistent look from [2.3](#23-visual-brand-and-editing-style-the-exterior), applied to each video.
- **How to apply it:** Edit every video in your chosen colors, fonts and visual types. Add music that suits the mood,
  and for stories add an **emotional background song**. Look at other storytelling videos to see what lands.
- **Example** *(illustrative)*: Hooks always appear in your primary font in brand orange, captions are white
  in the secondary font, and every tip gets a screenshot.

## Part 6: Storytelling content

*Source: #3, supported by #2, #5, #8 and #13*

### 6.1 Why storytelling is now required
- **What it is:** You used to be able to grow on educational content alone. To build a **community** rather than a
  following, people now need to know, like and trust you, and storytelling does that. Stories are more relatable and,
  in Ava's experience, are converting to followers and leads better than some educational posts.
- **How to apply it:** Always post a **mix** of educational and storytelling content.
- **Example:** Whether you're selling a product, service or book, or just building a brand, #3 presents storytelling
  as now "required" to get meaningful results.

### 6.2 The seven story types
- **What it is / example:**

  | Story type | What it is | Example hook from #3 |
  |---|---|---|
  | My story | Life story or founder story | "I started my pajama business when I was 19 with $800." |
  | Win | Any achievement, milestone, opportunity or dream reached | "This is how my most viral video changed my life." |
  | Loss | A mistake, failure or bad result | "Last year I sold my business for $52M. Here's every idea I tried before my big win." |
  | Lesson | What a win or loss taught you | "I've been on eight dates this year, and here's the one trait I learned you need." |
  | Transformation | Your own or a client's before and after | "10 years: from living in a car to living in this penthouse." |
  | Challenge | A challenge you set and your journey through it | "I wrote a letter to the leader of every country in the world. Here's how many responded." |
  | Big goal / dream journey | Announcing a big goal and the plan to reach it | "My goal is for my candy brand to pass Sour Patch Kids in Instagram followers. Here's my plan." |

- **How to apply it:** Brainstorm a list of stories for each type, then pick the strongest.

### 6.3 Writing the story script
- **What it is:** A story follows a structure, just like a movie or TV episode. Ava keeps one or more templates per story type.
- **How to apply it:**
  1. Find a 5x outlier of the **same story type**, then transcribe and templatize it ([5.5](#55-script-structures)).
  2. Write your script **line by line, in bullets**, using the template.
  3. Use a viral 5x hook for the verbal and written hooks.
- **Example:** In #3, Ava writes a script for a fictional fitness coach who lost 50 lbs in college:
  - *Hook* (the "Is it possible" template): "Is it possible to lose 50 lbs as a broke, busy college kid in 6 months…?"
  - *The 3-step plan:*
    1. Find maintenance calories with a TDEE calculator and subtract 500.
    2. Build a meal plan that hits 100 g of protein a day.
    3. Lift 5 days a week and walk 10,000 steps daily.
  - *Result:* down 50 lbs, and the experience led to founding a coaching company.
  - *CTA:* "Follow so we can motivate each other."

### 6.4 Best formats for stories
- **What it is:** Three formats work especially well for stories:
  - **voiceover**;
  - **shot / angle change**;
  - **audio B-roll + text**, which shows a new image or clip every 1 to 3 seconds, one per story line, over an emotional soundtrack.
- **How to apply it:** Pick one and adjust the script to fit it.
- **Example** *(illustrative)*: A founder story told over old photos, a new image for each line, with soft piano music.

### 6.5 Plan the visuals
- **What it is:** The best story videos show **real past photos or videos** from the time being described.
- **How to apply it:** Use the color-coded script document ([7.4](#74-the-color-coded-script-document)). Add red notes for
  shots to film and green notes linking to past clips or photos for the editor. Add an emotional song in the edit.
- **Example:** A storytelling script in #3 where each bullet has a red shot note or a green link to an old photo or video.

## Part 7: The production system

### 7.1 Posting amount and schedule
*Sources: #2, #12*

- **What it is:** Choose a posting amount of **easy, moderate or hard** (the exact numbers were on screen only) and a
  schedule. Which days you post doesn't matter; the schedule is there for accountability.
- **How to apply it:** Choose a level you can sustain, fix your posting days, and plan each monthly batch around them.
- **Example** *(illustrative)*: "Moderate" at 4 reels a week, posted Monday, Wednesday, Friday and Sunday.

### 7.2 Batch a month of content in about 60 minutes
*Sources: #2, #4, #12*

- **What it is:** A monthly system that turns content creation from a messy daily scramble into a business process,
  so you don't burn out or run out of ideas.
- **How to apply it:**
  1. **Account type:** decide whether you're a new or an established account. This sets your ratio in [7.3](#73-the-702010-and-9010-rules).
  2. **Posting amount and schedule** ([7.1](#71-posting-amount-and-schedule)).
  3. **Niche keywords** ([4.2](#42-the-research-keyword-list)).
  4. **Research** ([4.3](#43-the-research-methods)).
  5. **Ideation document**, filled in this order for every video:
     - **hook stack**, using hooks from *different* outliers;
     - **value**, part 1: the story or knowledge from your own head;
     - **value**, part 2: how the script is structured (step-by-step, list, ranking, story, or a structure from your bank);
     - **CTA**: follow, ManyChat, engagement, or none;
     - **format**.
  6. **Script, film, edit and upload** on schedule, then repeat every month.

  Batch anywhere from one week to one month, but **never more than a month**.
- **Example** *(illustrative)*: On the first Monday of each month: 20 minutes of research, 25 minutes filling 16 rows of the
  ideation doc, and 15 minutes assigning CTAs and formats. Scripting happens the next day.

### 7.3 The 70/20/10 and 90/10 rules
*Sources: #2, #12, credited to Caleb*

- **What it is:** A content mix for each monthly batch.
  - **Established accounts (70/20/10):**
    - 70%: what's proven, meaning hooks and topics from niche outliers;
    - 20%: **double downs** on your own best videos;
    - 10%: brand-new experiments of your own.
  - **New accounts (90/10):** with nothing of your own to double down on yet, it's 90% proven and 10% experiments.
- **How to apply it:** Split the month's video count by the right ratio before ideating.
- **Example** *(illustrative)*:
  - An established account posting 20 a month makes 14 from outliers, 4 double downs and 2 experiments.
  - A new account posting 20 makes 18 from outliers and 2 experiments.

### 7.4 The color-coded script document
*Sources: #2, #3*

- **What it is:** A script layout that makes filming and editing fast.
- **How to apply it:** Write the **format** at the top, then each sentence as a bullet:
  - **Black** bullet: the script line.
  - **Red** sub-bullet: the shot or action to film for that line.
  - **Green** sub-bullet: an editing note for the editor, such as an effect, text, or a link to a past clip or photo.
- **Example** *(illustrative)*:
  - (black) "Six months ago I had zero clients."
    - (red) Close-up, sitting on the floor with laptop.
    - (green) Add the old calendar screenshot; zoom in on the empty weeks.

### 7.5 Filming
*Source: #2*

- **What it is:** A simple, cheap setup and a file system that prevents chaos.
- **How to apply it:**
  - *Gear:* a phone, a cheap tripod and a cheap microphone. You don't need a fancy camera.
  - *Filming:* film one bullet at a time; the editor cuts out the pauses.
  - *Files:* make one folder per batch with one subfolder per short. Upload each video's clips into its folder **right after filming it**,
    not all at the end.
- **Example:** Ava films everything on an iPhone with a tripod and mic bought on Amazon (#2, #3).

### 7.6 Editing
*Sources: #2, #3*

- **What it is:** Turning the raw clips into the final video using the script notes.
- **How to apply it:** Business owners should hire an editor and hand over the color-coded script plus clips. Before that,
  CapCut and YouTube tutorials are enough.
- **Example:** Ava edited early videos in CapCut before hiring an editor, and admits they "were not good" but got the job done (#2).

### 7.7 Uploading and captions
*Sources: #2, #3*

- **What it is:** A simple caption formula. Captions don't really drive performance.
- **How to apply it:**
  1. Line 1: the written hook.
  2. A blank line.
  3. Line 2: the CTA.
  4. A blank line.
  5. Line 3: 3 to 5 hashtags covering your niche, target audience and sub-topic. They act as keywords that help reach the
     right people, not as a performance boost.
- **Example** *(illustrative)*:
  > How to dress for your body type
  >
  > Comment STYLE for my free body-shape guide
  >
  > #stylingtips #bodyshape #womensfashion

## Part 8: Growth roadmap: 0 to 100K

### 8.1 Level 1: 0 to 1,000 followers
*Source: #2*

- **What it is:** The hardest stage, where most people give up.
- **How to apply it:**
  1. Personal brand basics ([2.1](#21-niche-and-sub-niches-the-foundation) to [2.3](#23-visual-brand-and-editing-style-the-exterior)).
  2. Optimize your profile ([2.4](#24-profile-optimization-the-top-half)).
  3. Set a posting amount and schedule.
  4. Script shorts with the 7 factors, batching a week to a month at a time.
  5. Film.
  6. Edit.
  7. Upload.
  8. Repeat steps 4 to 7 until you reach 1K, typically after **50 to 100 videos**.
- **Example:** It took Ava about 2 weeks to get a first hit on a personal account, and about 3 months on a brand-new business account.

### 8.2 Level 2: 1,000 to 10,000 followers
*Source: #2*

- **What it is:** You now have data, so you start learning from your own page.
- **How to apply it:**
  1. **Study** your top 5 and bottom 5 videos. Instagram's **Edits** app shows this: connect your account, open the menu,
     and look at best and worst performing.
  2. **Double down** on your outliers ([8.4](#84-doubling-down-templatizing-your-viral-outliers)).
  3. Batch monthly with **70/20/10** ([7.3](#73-the-702010-and-9010-rules)).
  4. Repeat until you reach 10K.
- **Example** *(illustrative)*: Your top 5 are all "do vs don't" clone videos, and your bottom 5 are all sit-down talking. Next month leans into clones.

### 8.3 Level 3: 10,000 to 100,000 followers
*Source: #2*

- **What it is:** The same loop as Level 2, which #2 admits is "super boring", with one critical check: are you building a
  personal brand or just a following ([1.1](#11-build-a-personal-brand-not-a-following))?
- **How to apply it:** Keep running monthly 70/20/10 batches. Make sure every month has educational, storytelling and authority
  content, not one type or fluffy trends.
- **Example:** Comparing two creators, the one with a following sees wildly fluctuating views, and the one with a personal brand
  gets consistent views on every post (#2).

### 8.4 Doubling down: templatizing your viral outliers
*Sources: #2, #5, #7, #8, #12*

- **What it is:** When smart creators get a viral video, they **remake it again and again with small tweaks**.
- **How to apply it:**
  1. **Find your outliers**, the videos far above your average (for example 10K+ views when you average 1K).
  2. **Analyze** each one by breaking it into the 7 factors.
  3. **Remake it**, carrying some factors over into the new video:
     - **A:** keep most factors and make a small change to the value;
     - **B:** keep the format or structure and change everything else;
     - **C:** keep everything and change **only the format**, which is underrated and very effective.
- **Example:** Ava's 90-day doubling-down experiment produced more than 12M views and about 83,000 new followers.

## Part 9: AI in your content workflow

*Source: #11*

### 9.1 The three levels of AI
- **What it is:**
  1. **LLMs** (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok, Perplexity): you ask and they answer. This is where most people are.
  2. **AI agents** (Claude Code, Claude Cowork, Codex): they execute the workflows and tasks you define, like an SOP.
  3. **Agentic AI** (tools like OpenClaw and Manus): they plan their own multi-step systems, adapt, and correct their own mistakes.

  You'll use a mix of all three, and levels 2 and 3 will become as normal as ChatGPT within a few years.
- **How to apply it:** Pick 1 to 3 go-to LLMs, since each is better at different things, and 1 to 2 agent tools. Keeping everything in one tool is easier.
- **Example:** Ava uses:
  - **Claude**;
  - **Acquisition AI**, an LLM trained on Alex Hormozi's content;
  - **Claude Cowork / Claude Code / OpenClaw** for agents;
  - **Wispr Flow** dictation, which is said to save about an hour of typing a day.

### 9.2 Educate the AI
- **What it is:** Give the AI full context on you before asking it for anything.
- **How to apply it:**
  1. Introduce yourself, your business, your role, your goals, and what you want from the AI.
  2. Ask whether it has any clarifying questions.
  3. Upload your **best** past work as data sources, and only high-quality examples.
- **Example** *(illustrative)*: Upload your 10 best-performing scripts, your offer page and your brand guide.

### 9.3 The master prompt
*Credited to Dan Martell*

- **What it is:** A saved document that describes you and your role, which you attach to every important prompt.
- **How to apply it:**
  1. Prompt: *"I want to create a master prompt for my role as [role]. Ask me all the questions you need to know."*
  2. Answer its questions and refine anything that's off.
  3. Save it as a PDF in Google Drive, named something like "Master prompt – [position]".
  4. Include it whenever you ask the LLM something.
- **Example** *(illustrative)*: "Master prompt – Founder & content lead" covers your audience, offer, tone, banned phrases and best-performing topics.

### 9.4 Prompting basics
- **What it is:** Four habits:
  1. **AI hygiene:** start a new chat for every topic (credited to Hormozi).
  2. **Iterate:** several rounds in the same chat produce better output than one shot.
  3. **Quality in, quality out** (credited to Dan Martell).
  4. **The prompt formula:** Role → Context → Command → Format.
- **How to apply it:** Write every prompt with all four parts of the formula, and keep refining inside the same chat.
- **Example** *(illustrative)*: "You are a short-form scriptwriter (role). Here is my master prompt and a 5x outlier transcript
  (context). Write 3 scripts using this structure with my own client story (command). Give each as bullets with a hook stack at the
  top (format)."

### 9.5 Break roles into workflows and actions
*Credited to Hormozi*

- **What it is:** Before automating, map your work: **roles** contain **workflows**, and workflows contain **actions** (think of each action as a mouse click).
- **How to apply it:**
  1. Pick one role.
  2. List all its workflows.
  3. List every action in each workflow from start to finish.

  If you already have SOPs, you're far ahead, because they're already written this way.
- **Example:** An admin-assistant role might have about 15 workflows, such as uploading content, answering emails and paying invoices.
  Uploading a month of content to Meta might take about 200 clicks.

### 9.6 Skills → agents → a chained workflow
- **What it is:** In Claude, each workflow or SOP becomes a **skill markdown file**, and each skill gets one **agent**. A role with
  five workflows ends up with five or more agents, chained so one agent's output is the next one's input.
- **How to apply it:**
  1. Turn each SOP into a skill file.
  2. **Test** it on a real task, see where it breaks, and correct it the way you'd train a new employee. Big processes can take more than 100 rounds.
  3. Once it does the work **as well as or better than you**, turn it into an agent.
  4. Map the **handoffs**: agent 1 outputs X, which agent 2 uses to produce Y, and so on.
  5. Connect roles to each other, so the whole business runs as one linear workflow.
- **Rules:**
  - Don't replace a task with AI until it does the work as well as you.
  - Be ultra-specific when correcting it. Instead of "be more authoritative", say "talk faster, raise your voice, puff your chest out."
- **Example:** An editor role with five SOPs becomes five skill files, then five agents chained into one editing pipeline.

### 9.7 What stays human
- **What it is:** Four things AI can't do for you:
  - **ideas and topics**, which are "still alpha";
  - your **stories and lived experiences**;
  - **human quality control**, such as checking edits for spelling and the wrong visuals;
  - your **decision-making**.
- **How to apply it:** Use AI to cut time, for example turning a 2-hour script into a 20-minute one, but put your own stories into
  every script, review every deliverable, and keep the decisions yourself.
- **Example:** An automated edit still needs a person to check for spelling mistakes and the wrong visuals before it's posted.

### 9.8 Standing out against "AI slop"
- **What it is:** Feeds are filling with identical videos (same hook, script, format and visuals) made by AI or by people copying each other.
  The winners will be people with **personal brands**.
- **How to apply it:** Six differentiators:
  1. Create your **own series**.
  2. Create your **own signature format** that you can double down on.
  3. Put **social proof** in your scripts, meaning your results and client results, which nobody can copy.
  4. Add **personal stories and experiences**.
  5. Add **your personality**, so you don't sound like AI.
  6. Be the **first** to post a viral idea in your niche. Use viral hooks and structures, but never copy a whole video.
- **Example** *(illustrative)*: A daily "Day 1 to Day 90 of launching my bakery" series with real revenue screenshots that no one else can recreate.
